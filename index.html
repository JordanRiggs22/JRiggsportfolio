The Pivot: Engineering → Business Analysis

A hybrid narrative — four years of applied engineering and program coordination, now redirected toward the strategic, data-driven side of the business.

My background is technical by training and cross-functional by necessity. At the Naval Air Warfare Center, I wasn't just running tests — I was reviewing technical deliverables, facilitating meetings, and coordinating across teams on Combat Integration & Identification Systems, the same muscles a business analyst uses to gather requirements and align stakeholders.

Independently, I taught myself to treat every dataset like a signal to be decoded: the Spotify streaming analysis and the SAR imaging system below both start from raw, noisy data and end in a clear, defensible conclusion. That habit — ambiguity in, insight out — is what I'm now formalizing through Wake Forest's MSBA and pointing at business problems instead of RF problems.

I'm not leaving engineering behind; I'm translating it. The result is a business analyst who can sit in a room with engineers and executives alike and be fluent in both.
